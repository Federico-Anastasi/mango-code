# Example: RTX 3060 Ti + GTX 1080 + GTX 1660 SUPER

Class **R**: holds for this hardware and for our patches. It serves as an example of how a setup was reached, not as a rule. The rules are in
[general.md](general.md). Sources: abbreviations in [README.md](README.md).

## Our patches (what they do and why; the `mango` branch of the forks mango-prism and mango-toq)

- **R1. `prism` (fork mango-prism, on the PrismML fork of llama.cpp b10709):** denies the `mma` kernels to cc 7.5 only (`GGML_CUDA_TU116`: 1660S from 264 to 1030
  tok/s of prefill, G40); CUDA graphs even on Pascal/Turing (`GGML_CUDA_GRAPHS_ANY_ARCH=1`, G44); the clock keeper (`GGML_CUDA_CLOCK_KEEPER=1`, G50);
  `LLAMA_MTP_UBATCH` (the draft's context no longer copies the ubatch, G72); MMVQ tables for Pascal/Turing and the geometry of ternary PQ2_0 (G45); the conversations' on-disk memory (`--cache-disk`).
- **R2. `toq` (fork mango-toq, on ik_llama.cpp 35e7256):** trellis weight types (TRL), int8 GEMM with tensor cores (prefill on the 3060 Ti alone 340 → 929 tok/s), fused
  GEMMs for Pascal/Turing (f32/h2, prefill ×1.9), GEMV with tensor cores of 3 columns, batched DeltaNet (4 requests together 50 → 77.5 tok/s), q8 attention for GQA (`fattn-q8-gqa`), prefill on the main GPU
  with weights streamed from pinned RAM (R10), MTP, explicit layer map (`LLAMA_LAYER_DEVICES`), on-disk memory, diagnostic switches for every kernel.
- **R3. The clock keeper is an idle warp on a minimum-priority stream that starts only if there was a graph in the last 0.5 s** (at idle the GPUs go back to 8-10 W instead of 160 W). It is not needed by engines that already have persistent
  kernels (trellis GEMV without graph, F40).
- **R4. Every patch has a switch** to return to the exact path (`TRL_MMQ=0`, `FA_Q8_GQA=0`, `QNEXT_MULTI=0`, `TRL_TC=0`) and a check that must give zero (perplexity: 6.9776 exact, 6.9789 `mmq`, 6.9799 `mmq3`, 6.9782 `prefill_main`).
- **R5. The automatic splitter (`split.rs`)** reproduces TOQ's measured map: system reserve 1100 MiB + margin 256 per GPU, main buffer 242 MiB, `prefill_main` 527, MTP 50; at most 2 GPUs (WDDM pinned
  RAM); minimum cc 6.1; context in steps of 4096, minimum 32768. Here the hand-made map wins: with MTP the automatic mode would choose the 1080 instead of the 1660S.

## Other facts about this hardware

- **R10. Prefill on the main GPU (`toq` patch):** the weights of the other GPUs' layers travel through pinned RAM to the 3060 Ti (12.9 GB/s; the 1660S on x1 does 0.8), two slots in turn hidden under the compute.
  3060+1660S: 136 → 664/757/649/508 tok/s at 1k/32k/64k/120k. Below 128 tokens it stays on the 1660S (`LLAMA_PREFILL_MAIN_MIN=16`: 50 tok/s on 57 tokens, ~104 with 16). With 1080+1660 and several prompts >128 tokens together it hung.
- **R11. Context on this hardware:** the 3060 Ti alone holds ~14k with TOQ (weights 6326 + buffer 130 MiB); the 1660S alone does not fit; 120k on two GPUs. 3.6 at 128k leaves ~1 GB in total; 3.8 stops at 80k with MTP.
- **R12. The 26 Oct bench on 3.6 (21 successful starts, 24 "does not start")** shows how narrow the margin is: the road to making the draft fit at 128k went through a trial build of the engine that read `LLAMA_MTP_UBATCH`, draft KV f16 and the pipeline off.
- **R13. Open limits:** TOQ does not read images (random text for any image, the encoder on GPU crashes in `ggml_cuda_op_upscale`); saving 3.8's memory takes 15.8 s for 2.4 GB against ~3 s for Bonsai and LFM; restarting 3.6 on 3 GPUs
  prints `cudaMalloc failed 837.13 MiB on device 0 … retrying without pipeline parallelism` and starts all the same; TOQ-v1's quality not measured (MMLU-Redux stopped at 753/5700, estimate 87.4 ± 1.2).

## Timeline of the turning points (the measurement that changed course)

1. **17 Jul 2026 — from our own engine to llama.cpp.** A GLM engine written from scratch, streaming from NVMe, reached 0.22 tok/s (+12% over llama.cpp): the bottleneck was the disk, not the compute ("new hardware is needed"). We switched to a 35 B MoE (3 B active)
   that fits entirely on the GPUs with llama.cpp. *Lesson: the bottleneck is measured end-to-end, not from the micro-bench.*
2. **25 Aug — a single layer off the GPU.** 3.8 at 128k sent a layer to the CPU: the fused DeltaNet kernel switched off for the whole model (10.1 → 3.2 tok/s). Hence `-ngl 999` mandatory and 80k.
3. **25 Aug — the 1660S has no tensor cores.** Prefill on the 1660S alone was 264 tok/s against 1383 for the 1080: llama.cpp treated it as Turing. The TU116 patch takes it to 1030 (3.9x): 3.8 from 140 to 347, 3.6 from 471 to 1014. *Lesson: the
   first measurement is per GPU, not per model.*
4. **25 Aug — MTP was already there.** The first hypothesis ("the engine does not use the MTP head") was wrong: `--spec-type draft-mtp` uses it; 3.8 at 64k from 9.1 to 19.7 tok/s. The first measurements had ±20% because the text changed: hence T=0.
5. **8 Oct — the noise was the clocks.** A round gave 31.70 ± 3.35 tok/s; with the clock keeper 42.0 steady (+27-39%). Hence the keeper in every engine with a layer split.
6. **19 Oct — the CPU engine decides.** On CPU ik_llama.cpp `-rtr -muge` prefilled 3.5x llama.cpp and decoded +20-25%, more than all the "squeezing" of tuning.
7. **26 Oct — the 3.6 draft does not fit at 128k: three causes together.** CUDA declares ~0.3 GB less per GPU than is allocated, the automatic split puts the last layer, output and draft on the 1660S, the draft's context copies the ubatch. Three remedies
   (`-ts` in layers, `LLAMA_MTP_UBATCH=32`, draft KV f16): 118k from 16.7 to ~29-31 tok/s.
8. **28-29 Oct — prefill is limited by the weakest GPU.** 3060 Ti + 1660S: 136 tok/s with the 3060 working 5% of the time. The weights travel to the strong GPU: 508-757 tok/s. The same night MTP went from 3-6% acceptance to 92% (an allocator bug,
   not the model's) and the keeper turned out to be harmful on the persistent GEMV.
9. **30 Oct — 16 GB laptop: the count of bytes.** 83% of the bytes read per token are always-active parts: requantizing them to IQ4_XS gives +16% at 2 bits (forecast +23%) and +28/+40% at 4 bits (indicative).
10. **4-5 Nov — one engine fewer and on-disk memory.** The app's bench shows that 3.8 on the fork with the keeper beats the old build (24.2/16.7 against 21.5/16.3 at 1k/32k) and the stock build leaves the installer. On-disk memory takes the resumption of a 109k-token conversation
    from 6-8 minutes to 2.9 s.

## Known contradictions in the sources (not reconciled)

- 3.8 compute buffer: "~830/616/470 MiB at ub 512/256/128" (25 Aug) against "~110 MiB at ub 128, ~256 at ub 256".
- The 3.6 series are not comparable: `llama-bench` (30.3 / 19.6 / 14.7 on empty/64k/128k with the old engine), server with short text (28.0 → 40.2), `mango-bench` (61.6 / 49.5 / 39.5). They measure different things.
- The clock keeper on 3.8: "+12%" at 1k, +2.5% at 32k.
- MTP verification on TOQ: ×1.44 on the 3060 and ×1.8 on 3060+1660 (RIS) against "~1.4 on the 1660" (MOT).
- 3.8 with f16 KV: "does not fit" (`_fa`), "fits but does not gain" (`_da_provare`), +17-34% in `llama-bench` (BP).
