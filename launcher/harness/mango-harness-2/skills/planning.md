desc: Write a plan worth following, before a job of more than three steps

# Planning
A plan is where you do the thinking, while the files are in front of you. Later you will only have the step you are on: what is not written in it, you will not remember.

1. Look first. `search` and `read` everything the job touches. A plan made without looking is a guess.
2. Decide. Where there are two ways to do it, choose one now and write why in one line. A choice that is the user's: `ask` before you write the plan.
3. Cut the job into steps. A step is one change you can check on its own. If you cannot say how you will check it, it is not a step yet.
4. Write each step so that it can be done without looking again:
   - which files, and where in them (the function, the section, the line you saw);
   - what to change, with the names and the values, not "update the code";
   - the command or the view that proves it, and what it must answer.
5. Write `# Not doing`: what is near the request and was not asked for.
6. Send it with `plan`. Tell the user the titles of the steps in a few lines, then start from step 1.
7. After each step: check it, then `plan` with `done`. Read the step it gives you before you act.
8. A step that turns out wrong, or one that is missing: correct the plan first, with `plan` giving `step` and its new `text`, then go on. A step no longer needed: rewrite it as `Not needed:` and why, and mark it done. Don't drift from the plan in silence.

A plan of five lines is a list of wishes. A good step is five to fifteen lines.
